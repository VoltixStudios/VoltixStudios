Loom

Every day, one pattern.

You know all the moves. Can you find the order?

A plain woven cloth, the pattern it must become, and the four moves that get there: slide a row, swap two columns, mirror the cloth, turn it. Every move is given. What is not given is the order. Three attempts (five on Sundays), one puzzle a day, the same for everyone in the world, and a small grid of squares to share that spoils nothing.

Two to five minutes, one thumb, no words to read. Warm, quiet, and finished on the bus.

⟶
Play today's cloth

  • One cloth a day

    The puzzle is a pure function of the date. Everyone weaves the same cloth, and the only way to play again is tomorrow.

  • Order, not content

    Every operation is on the table from the first second. Nothing to find, nothing to guess at: only the sequence is hidden, and each weave tells you which slots were right.

  • Harder by weekday

    Monday is plain. Midweek the loom says only how many slots were right, not which. Friday hides a decoy tile that belongs nowhere. Saturday has five slots. Sunday has all of it on a bigger cloth.

  • The quilt

    Every day's final cloth, solved or not, is sewn into your quilt, a month at a time. Thirty days is a quilt, and it can be shared as a picture.

  • A streak that never threatens

    It counts days played, not days solved. A lost day keeps it. Nothing ever tells you not to break it.

  • Nothing to buy to play

    The daily cloth, the last week of the archive, a free hint a day, the statistics and the quilt are free. Loom Full, once, buys every cloth ever published and no advertising, ever.
